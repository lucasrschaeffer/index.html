Our Mission

Two goals.
One walk.

Pathfinders NYC exists because two things are true at once: our neighbors on the streets of downtown Manhattan need real, tangible help — and the teenagers in our own city have more energy, empathy, and capacity to lead than anyone gives them credit for. We're trying to put those two facts in the same room.

We don't believe in charity that strips people of their dignity. Every walk is a lesson in "street-side manner" — the same principle that guides a good clinician: approach with curiosity, listen first, and treat every person as fully human. We model this, practice it together, and debrief on it after every shift.

The science is clear: loneliness and isolation drive mental illness, substance use, and deteriorating health. A 2024 Harvard study found that 81% of chronically lonely adults reported anxiety or depression. Genuine human connection — stopping, talking, seeing someone — is itself an act of public health. Our goal isn't just to hand out supplies. It's to build a city where people are seen.

Pathfinders NYC is entirely secular, entirely grassroots, and built to run lean. Every dollar raised goes directly into kits. Every volunteer hour counts toward your school's community service requirement. No bureaucracy. No barriers. Just show up and step up.

Trusted Routes, Real Roots

Our routes aren't new. They're the same paths Lucas walked as a high schooler with the Downtown Hunger Action Project (DHAP). Pathfinders NYC is a direct continuation of that work — updated, expanded, and built to last. Entirely secular. Open to everyone.

Bard HSEC Manhattan, Class of 2024
Founder

Hi, I'm Lucas.

Coming home to New York from Nashville this past year, I was struck by something I'd somehow stopped noticing — the number of people sleeping on the streets downtown. It hit differently when I was seeing it fresh.

During high school, I was part of the Downtown Hunger Action Project. We walked the same routes every week, handing out food and clothing to unhoused neighbors. It was one of the most formative things I did growing up here. When I came back and looked for something similar for local teenagers to join over the summer, I couldn't find anything. So I made it.

The research backs up what I felt intuitively. A 2024 Harvard study found that 81% of lonely adults reported anxiety or depression — compared to 29% of those who felt more connected. The U.S. Surgeon General declared loneliness a public health epidemic in 2023. People experiencing homelessness face these pressures at disproportionate levels. Meaningful human contact isn't a nice-to-have. It genuinely matters.

My hope is that Pathfinders can be two things at once: a real, tangible resource for unhoused neighbors, and proof that a group of teenagers stopping to say good evening makes a difference — for the city, and for the people doing the stopping.

How a shift actually works

01
Pre-Walk · 20 min

Prep & Huddle

Volunteers meet at the rally point, assemble care kits as a team, and gather for a structured pre-walk huddle with your peer mentor.

Street-side manner coaching — how to approach, greet, and listen
Safety briefing, cohort pairing, route overview
02
The Walk · ~90 min

Walk the Route

Small, supervised cohorts of 4–6 teens walk a proven ~2-mile downtown route alongside a trained mentor. Every person we meet receives both a food bag and a clean kit — distributed with dignity.

Cohorts of 4–6 volunteers, never alone
Mentor-supervised at all times
Proven, well-trafficked public routes (used previously with DHAP)
Focus on listening and human connection, not just drop-off

Debrief & Reflect

Every shift ends with a structured debrief — because processing what you saw matters as much as what you did. This is where leadership is built.

For Teens (Ages 13+)

If you go to school anywhere in NYC and you spend time downtown — this is for you. No experience needed. You don't need to know what you're doing, you don't need to be a "volunteer type." You just need to want to show up.

Every shift earns 4 verified service hours accepted by NYC public and private high schools. Your first walk is free — try it before committing. After that, a one-time $50 join fee covers your participation for the rest of the summer (your portion of kit supplies, training materials, and a Pathfinders tote). Small, supervised cohorts — 4 to 6 teens per mentor at all times. Your child is never unaccompanied.
Proven public routes in well-trafficked downtown neighborhoods — used previously by Lucas with the Downtown Hunger Action Project (DHAP).
Parent/guardian waiver required. We collect emergency contacts and communicate shift details in advance.
Founded and led by a pre-med college student with hands-on public health experience at the NYC Department of Health, supported by a vetted team of college-aged cohort leaders.
Optional parent observation — parents are welcome to attend their child's first shift to see how the program runs in person.
Scheduled shifts shared in advance — typically Friday evenings, Saturday mornings, or Saturday evenings. Parents confirm each shift before their teen participates.

FAQ

Questions we actually get.

Organized by who's asking. Tap any question to expand it.

For teens
Do I need any experience or special skills?
None at all. You don't need to have volunteered before, you don't need to know anyone in the program, and you don't need to be a "community service type." The pre-walk huddle gets everyone on the same page before we go. You show up — we handle the rest.
Can I come alone, or do I need to bring a friend?
Either works. Lots of volunteers come solo and meet people through the program. Bringing a friend is great too — cohorts are 4–6 people, so there's room. Just make sure you both sign up separately.
How do I get my service hours verified for school?
After every shift, Lucas signs a service-hour confirmation for each volunteer — 4 hours per shift. Most NYC schools accept this directly. If your school requires a specific form or letter, tell us before your first walk and we'll fill it out correctly. After your third shift we can also write a summary letter for college applications.
What should I wear and bring?
Comfortable walking shoes — we cover about 2 miles. Dress for the weather; sunscreen and a water bottle are smart in summer. Don't bring anything valuable you'd be stressed about losing. We supply everything we're distributing.
How does the $50 join fee work? Is it really free to try?
Yes — your first walk is genuinely free. Come, see what a shift looks like, decide if it's right for you. If you want to keep coming, a one-time $50 fee covers your participation for the rest of the summer (your share of kit supplies, training materials, and a Pathfinders tote). One payment, all season. You only pay if you're in.
What if I can't afford the $50 fee?
Reach out to Lucas directly. We don't want money to be the reason someone misses out — there's a need-based waiver available, no questions asked, no documentation required. Just email lucasrschaeffer@gmail.com.
What if I have to cancel after signing up?
Life happens. Just email or text Lucas as early as possible so we can adjust cohort sizes. No penalty — sign up for a future date whenever you're ready.
When do shifts actually run?
Most weekends throughout the summer. Typical slots are Friday evenings, Saturday mornings, and Saturday evenings — we vary the schedule so volunteers can find times that work. Each shift's exact date and time is posted in advance, and you only sign up for the specific shifts you want.
What if something uncomfortable happens on the walk?
Your mentor is trained specifically for this. If an interaction gets hard — someone is in crisis, someone is aggressive, or you just feel unsure — step back and let your mentor handle it. The post-walk debrief is designed to process anything that felt difficult. You will never be left to handle something alone.
For cohort leaders (18+)
Do I need public health or social work experience?
It helps, but it's not required. We're looking for maturity, reliability, and genuine interest in working with teens and unhoused communities. Lucas trains every cohort leader directly before their first shift — street-side manner, safety protocols, and how to facilitate a debrief.
What's the actual time commitment?
One 4-hour shift per weekend (or as your schedule allows), plus a one-time training session before your first walk. Flexible around classes and travel. One Friday or Saturday a week doing something that matters — not a 40-hour commitment.
For parents & guardians
Who exactly is supervising my child?
Every cohort is led by a trained, background-checked college student. Lucas Schaeffer — the program founder — personally runs every walk this first summer. He's a Bard HSEC Manhattan alum ('24), rising junior at Vanderbilt studying Medicine, Health & Society, and has worked at the NYC Department of Health. He grew up in downtown Manhattan.
Can I come and see how the program works?
Yes — and we actively encourage it. Parents are welcome to attend any shift as observers, no advance notice needed. Come for the first one, see how the huddle runs, meet the other teens and mentors. We'd rather you see it in person than wonder.
What areas does the program walk through?
Routes go through downtown Manhattan on well-trafficked streets. These aren't new — they're the same paths Lucas walked as a high schooler with the Downtown Hunger Action Project (DHAP). Pathfinders NYC was born directly out of that experience. Exact route and timing for each shift are shared with families in advance.
My child has a medical condition or allergy. Can they still participate?
In most cases, yes. The waiver includes a section for you to note any conditions, allergies, or medications we should know about. If you have specific concerns, reach out to Lucas directly before signing up — he'd rather have that conversation upfront.
Is this affiliated with any religious organization?
No. Pathfinders NYC is entirely secular and not affiliated with any religious institution, political organization, or corporate sponsor. We welcome volunteers of every background, faith, and identity.
Donations & money
Where does my donation actually go?
Directly into kits — food bags (sandwich, water, granola bar, clementine) and clean kits (soap, razor, toothbrush, toothpaste, socks). Volunteer join fees cover volunteer participation; outside donations let us reach more neighbors and waive fees for teens who can't afford to pay. No salaries, no office costs, no overhead. Full expense breakdowns available on request.
Is my donation tax-deductible?
Not yet. Pathfinders NYC is a grassroots initiative in its first year and not yet incorporated as a 501(c)(3). If tax-deductibility matters, the most helpful thing right now is sharing the program with families who need service hours, or buying supplies directly off our kit wishlist.
Can I sponsor a specific shift rather than donating generally?
Yes — email Lucas at lucasrschaeffer@gmail.com and we can arrange it. Sponsoring a full shift's kit supply (typically $50–100 depending on group size) funds one complete walk, start to finish. We'll send you a note on how it went.
